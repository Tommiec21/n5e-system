# Chapter 3: Ambitions & Background

Characters are defined by much more than their clan and class. They’re individuals with their own stories, interests, connections, and capabilities beyond those that class and species defined. This section expounds on the details that distinguish characters from one another, including the basics of name and physical description and the rules of backgrounds.

### Character Details

Your character’s name and physical description might be the first things that other players learn about your character. It’s worth thinking about how these characteristics reflect the character you have in mind.

#### Name

Your name will be the title you give yourself and the title everyone will call you throughout the campaign. Put some thought into your name even if you’re just picking one from a list.

#### Sex

You can play a male or female character without gaining any special benefits or hindrances. Think about how your character does or does not conform to the broader culture’s expectations of sex and gender. You don’t need to be confined to binary notions of sex and gender. Some people believe in companionship that doesn’t take into account gender. You could also play a female character who presents herself as a man or a man who feels trapped in a female body. Likewise, your character’s sexual orientation is for you to decide.

#### Height and Weight

You can decide your character’s height and weight, using the information provided in your species description. Think about what your character’s ability scores might say about his or her height and weight. A weak but agile character might be thin. A strong and durable character might be tall or heavy.

#### Other Physical Characteristics

You choose your character’s age and the color of their hair, eyes, and skin. To add a touch of distinctiveness, you might want to give your character an unusual or memorable physical characteristic, such as a scar, a limp, or a tattoo.

### Personal Characteristics

Your shinobi is more than their clan and class. Their personality, habits, beliefs, and the things that push them forward are what make them a person rather than a character sheet. The four characteristics below help you define who your shinobi is, what they want, and why in matters to them.

Each background presented later in this chapter serves as a starting point for thinking through these characteristics. Think of your background as your shinobi's origin before they ever put on a headband..

#### Ambition

Your shinobi's **Ambition** is their long-term goal, the thing they are ultimately working toward. **Ambitions** should be grand in scale: becoming Kage, earning a legendary reputation across the shinobi world, or seeking vengeance against a powerful enemy. This is the destination your shinobi is always moving toward, even when the path is long and unclear.

An **Ambition** should be broad enough that there are many ways to pursue it, but specific enough to feel real and personal. One or two sentences is usually enough. It should be something others could learn about and react to, whether that reaction is disbelief, admiration, or a shared sense of rivalry.

#### Drive

Your shinobi's **Drive** is the reason they keep going. It is the personal motivation beneath every decision they make and every hardship they push through. Unlike an Ambition, which describes what your shinobi wants, a **Drive** describes why they want it.

A **Drive** should feel deeply personal and remain consistent. It does not change unless your shinobi achieves their Ambition or something fundamental in their life shifts dramatically. Even when your shinobi is uncertain, exhausted, or outmatched, their **Drive** is what pulls them back to their feet.

#### Goals

**Goals** are the short-term objectives your shinobi is actively working toward. Unlike an Ambition, Goals are achievable within a mission, arc, or session: completing a dangerous assignment, protecting someone important, mastering a new technique, or defeating a rival. They should require genuine effort and carry real stakes, but be concrete enough to actually accomplish.

**Goals** change often and should be updated as the story evolves. When your shinobi achieves a **Goal**, the GM should consider awarding them a Will of Fire in recognition of that accomplishment.

#### Fears

Every shinobi carries something they are afraid of. Failure, death, losing someone they love, or falling short of what the people around them expect. A **Fear** should be specific enough to feel grounded and personal, but not so narrow that it rarely surfaces in play.

**Fears** are not weaknesses to be hidden. They reveal something true about who your shinobi is, and the moments when they are forced to face them directly are often the most defining moments of their story.

### Will of Fire

Will of Fire is a rule the game master can use to reward you for playing your character in a way that’s true to his or her personality traits, ideal, bond, and flaws. By using the Will of Fire, you can draw on your personality trait of compassion for the downtrodden to give you an edge in negotiating with the Beggar Prince. Or inspiration can let you call on your bond to the defense of your home village to push past the effect of a power that has been laid on you.

#### Gaining the Will of Fire

All players begin any mission or session they undertake with a Will of Fire. Your GM can choose to delay providing a will of fire at the start of a session if you previously ended a session mid combat, or in a compromising situation where you didn’t have a Will of Fire before. They can also give you the will of fire for a variety of reasons. Typically, GMs award it when you play out your Ambitions, Drive, Goals or Fears and otherwise portray your character in a compelling way.

Also, once per encounter, the first time you roll a Natural 1 or a Natural 20, you gain a Will of Fire. A player can have up to 3 Will of Fire at any given time.

#### Using the Will of Fire

If you have the will of fire, you can expend it for the following.

Combat And Exploration

  - Advantage on an attack roll, saving throw or check.

  - Reroll an attack roll, saving throw or check.

  - Automatically succeed a Death saving throw.

  - Can immediately act using an ability, skill-based action or jutsu that requires an Action, Bonus Action or Reaction. If used other creatures turn, this act occurs as part of the turn creatures most recent activity (Attack, Cast a Jutsu, etc.), using best interpretation with the GM to resolve any possible inconsistencies or timing issues.

Social And Exploration

  - Give an allied creature your Will of Fire.

  - Gain insight or clue into the current situation or mystery.

  - To slightly alter the scene to your benefit.

### Backgrounds

Every story has a beginning. Your character’s background reveals where you came from, how you became a shinobi, and your place in the world. Your Scout-Nin might have been a Noble or a Street Urchin. Your Ninjutsu Specialist could have been a student of a prominent Master or a Prodigy. Your Weapon Specialist might have been a Hermit traveling with their family, place to place before settling down or entertained audiences as a carnival act.

Choosing a background provides you with important story beats about your character’s identity and most recent origin. The most important question to ask about your background is what changed? Why did you stop doing whatever your background describes to learn the way of Ninjutsu? How did you learn the skills of your class? What sets you apart from ordinary people who share your background

#### Proficiencies

Each background gives a character proficiency in two skills. If a character would gain the same proficiency from two different sources, they can choose a different proficiency of the same kind (skill or tool) instead.

#### Equipment

Each background provides a package of starting equipment.

#### Background Feature & Feat

Each background gives the character a background feature and a Feat.

Each background possesses a Recommended feat group to use based on the backgrounds narrative.

#### Suggested Characteristic

A background contains suggested personal characteristics based on your background. You can pick characteristics, roll dice to determine them randomly, or use the suggestions as inspiration for characteristics of your own creation.

#### Languages and Dialects

Common is the universal tongue of the shinobi world, spoken in every nation from the Land of Fire to the Land of Water. In its standard form it serves as a shared foundation that allows a merchant from Iron Country and a shinobi from the Hidden Sand to communicate without confusion.

Every country has developed its own regional dialect shaped by geography, culture, and centuries of isolation. These dialects share Common's core structure but diverge sharply in pronunciation, vocabulary, cadence, and idiom. A shinobi from the Land of Lightning and a shinobi from the Land of Wind are both speaking Common, but the rhythm, slang, and phrasing of one may be nearly unintelligible to the other.

Speaking standard Common cuts through this barrier entirely. A character fluent in standard Common can communicate clearly with anyone regardless of their regional dialect. A character who has only learned their home dialect, however, may find themselves lost in unfamiliar territory , understanding just enough to know they are being talked around.

In any case, you are frequently looked to for advice by someone with power. Your role is not necessarily one of fame and prestige, though it could be. You may even occasionally pen documents on your superior’s behalf – with or without their knowledge.. 

**Skill Proficiencies**: Persuasion, History

**Tool Proficiencies:** Forensics’ kit

**Equipment**: A set of Fine clothes, a notebook of important dates and times.

**Equipment Pack**: Captain’s Pack or Field Medics Pack (Choose one).

#### Feature: Position of Power

Your former position carries residual authority in the networks you once served. When dealing with bureaucrats, officials, or anyone within your previous superior's sphere of influence, you gain advantage on Persuasion and Intimidation checks. Once per long rest you can invoke your former position to request a reasonable favor from such a person without payment — access to restricted information, a letter of introduction, or the overlooking of a minor infraction — at the GM's discretion.
